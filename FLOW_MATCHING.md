# Flow Matching, from the continuity equation to SmolVLA's action expert

Written while building `Panda/SmolVLA/`. Companion to `ACT/README.md` (CVAE) and
`Diffusion/README.md` (DDPM). The point of this file is that I should be able to
re-derive the loss on a whiteboard, and defend every design choice, without
re-reading the paper.

Reference: SmolVLA, Shukor et al., arXiv:2506.01844, §3.1.
Upstream theory: Lipman et al. 2022 (Flow Matching), Liu et al. 2022 (Rectified
Flow), Esser et al. 2024 (SD3, the transformer instantiation), Black et al. 2024
(pi-0, the first VLA to use it).

---

## 0. Why bother, given I already built DDPM

My Diffusion Policy build learns `p(A | O)` by reversing a stochastic noising
process over 100 timesteps, and needs 10 DDIM steps at inference to be usable.
Flow matching learns the same conditional distribution but replaces the SDE with
a **deterministic ODE whose paths are straight lines**. Consequences that matter
for robotics:

1. **Few-step inference.** A straight path can be integrated accurately with very
   few Euler steps. SmolVLA uses 10 and calls it fixed; that is not a speed hack
   with a quality cost, it is what a straight path buys you. DDIM's 10 steps are
   an *approximation* of a curved reverse trajectory; flow matching's 10 steps are
   *nearly exact* for a path that was trained to be straight.
2. **No noise schedule to tune.** No betas, no cosine-vs-linear, no
   `clip_sample_range`, no `prediction_type` choice. The interpolation is one
   line of code. Everything I fought with in `Diffusion/config.py` under
   `--- diffusion process (DDPM) ---` simply does not exist here.
3. **A cleaner training signal.** The regression target is a *constant* per
   sample (see §4), independent of `t`. In DDPM the target `ε` is also constant,
   but the *scaling* of the signal-to-noise ratio across timesteps is what the
   beta schedule is desperately trying to balance. Here the path is uniform.

What it does *not* buy: it is still a conditional generative model trained by
regression, so it inherits every data-coverage limitation I hit with ACT and DP.
Flow matching will not invent recovery behaviour I never demonstrated.

## 1. The setup: probability paths and vector fields

Let `x ∈ R^d` be the thing I want to generate. For SmolVLA, `x = A_t`, an action
chunk of shape `(chunk_size, action_dim)` flattened, i.e. `d = 50 × 32`.

I want to transport a simple distribution `p_0 = N(0, I)` to the data
distribution `p_1 = q(A | O)`. Define a **probability path** `p_τ` for
`τ ∈ [0, 1]` interpolating the two, and a time-dependent **vector field**
`u_τ(x): R^d → R^d` that generates it, meaning: if I initialise a particle at
`x_0 ~ p_0` and integrate the ODE

```
dx/dτ = u_τ(x),        x(0) = x_0
```

then `x(τ) ~ p_τ` for all `τ`, so `x(1) ~ p_1`. Sampling becomes solving an ODE.

The link between `p_τ` and `u_τ` is the **continuity equation** (mass
conservation / transport equation):

```
∂p_τ/∂τ + ∇ · (p_τ u_τ) = 0
```

Read it as: probability mass is neither created nor destroyed, it is only pushed
around by the velocity field `u_τ`. This is the whole mathematical content. A
`u_τ` satisfying this equation for a given `p_τ` is said to *generate* that path.

> **Contrast with diffusion.** DDPM also has a continuous-time limit, but its
> path is generated by a *stochastic* differential equation, and its
> deterministic counterpart (the probability-flow ODE) has a vector field that
> depends on the score `∇ log p_τ(x)`. Score matching estimates that score. Flow
> matching skips the score and regresses the velocity field directly. They are
> two parameterisations of the same underlying object; flow matching's is the one
> where you get to *choose* the path.

## 2. The objective I want but cannot compute

The obvious thing: pick a target path `p_τ` with a known generating field `u_τ`,
and regress a network `v_θ` onto it.

```
L_FM(θ) = E_{τ ~ U[0,1], x ~ p_τ} ‖ v_θ(x, τ) − u_τ(x) ‖²
```

This is intractable for two reasons:

1. I have no closed form for `u_τ(x)`, the field that transports Gaussian noise
   to the *whole* data distribution. That is exactly the hard object.
2. I cannot even sample from `p_τ` in general.

This is the same wall score matching hits, and the fix has the same flavour:
condition on a single data point, where everything becomes trivial.

---

## 3. Conditional flow matching: the trick

Pick one data sample `x_1 ~ q`. Define a **conditional** path `p_τ(x | x_1)`
that starts at noise and ends at a delta on `x_1`. The simplest choice, and the
one everyone uses, is the straight line:

```
x_τ = τ x_1 + (1 − τ) ε,        ε ~ N(0, I)
```

At `τ=0` this is `N(0, I)`; at `τ=1` it is exactly `x_1`. Now differentiate:

```
u_τ(x_τ | x_1) = d x_τ / dτ = x_1 − ε
```

**The conditional vector field is a constant** (in `τ`, and in `x`). It is just
the displacement from the noise sample to the data sample. That is the entire
trick: I traded an unknown field over the whole distribution for a known
displacement vector per training pair.

The **Conditional Flow Matching** objective is then

```
L_CFM(θ) = E_{τ ~ U[0,1], x_1 ~ q, ε ~ N(0,I)} ‖ v_θ(x_τ, τ) − (x_1 − ε) ‖²
```

Every term is samplable. Two lines of code.

### 3.1 Why this is legitimate (the part worth being able to state)

`L_CFM ≠ L_FM`, but **∇_θ L_CFM = ∇_θ L_FM** (Lipman et al., Theorem 2). So
minimising the tractable objective finds the same optimum as minimising the
intractable one.

The intuition is the standard conditional-expectation / bias-variance argument.
Expand the marginal path as a mixture over data points,
`p_τ(x) = ∫ p_τ(x | x_1) q(x_1) dx_1`. Plug into the continuity equation and the
marginal field turns out to be the *posterior-weighted average* of the
conditional fields:

```
u_τ(x) = E_{x_1 ~ p(x_1 | x_τ = x)} [ u_τ(x | x_1) ]
```

And an L2 regression onto a random variable converges to that variable's
conditional expectation. So regressing `v_θ(x, τ)` onto the noisy per-sample
targets `x_1 − ε` makes it converge to exactly the average I actually wanted.
The extra loss is irreducible target variance, which is constant in `θ` and so
vanishes from the gradient.

**Interview trap:** "isn't the model just learning to average conflicting
targets, so won't it blur multimodal actions like an L1 regressor?" No, and the
reason is important. The averaging happens at *fixed `x_τ`*. Near `τ=1`, `x_τ`
is nearly clean, so the posterior `p(x_1 | x_τ)` collapses to a single mode and
the field is sharp. Near `τ=0`, `x_τ` is nearly pure noise and the field points
at the distribution's centre of mass, which is fine because that is where the
trajectory *should* start. The multimodality is resolved *along the integration
path*, not squashed into one output. A direct L1 regressor has no path and so has
nowhere to put the ambiguity.

The paper's Table 10 is the evidence, and it is worth reading carefully rather
than quoting the headline. Flow matching vs L1 chunk regression on the four
LIBERO suites (Spatial / Object / Goal / Long):

```
Flow matching   89  94  85  53   avg 80.25
Regression      92  85  86  38   avg 75.25
```

Regression actually *wins* on Spatial (92 vs 89) and Goal (86 vs 85). The entire
5-point average gap comes from LIBERO-Long (53 vs 38). That is the suite with
long-horizon, multi-stage tasks, which is exactly where action multimodality
bites: at a subtask boundary there are several valid continuations. So the honest
claim is not "flow matching is uniformly better", it is "flow matching's
advantage appears precisely where the action distribution is genuinely
multimodal, and is a wash where it is not." That matches what I concluded
comparing DP to ACT on my own task: the generative machinery pays off only when
multimodality is the actual bottleneck.

## 4. SmolVLA's instantiation, and the paper/code sign discrepancy

This bit tripped me up and is exactly the sort of thing to get right.

**The paper (§3.1)** writes the noisy chunk as

```
A^τ_t = τ A_t + (1 − τ) ε
```

so `τ = 1` is clean data (matching §3 above), and then states the target as

```
u(A^τ_t | A_t) = ε − A_t
```

But `d A^τ_t / dτ = A_t − ε`, which is the *negative* of their stated target.
The paper's labelling is internally inconsistent by a sign.

**The lerobot implementation** (`policies/smolvla/modeling_smolvla.py:784-786`)
uses the opposite time convention, inherited from pi-0/openpi:

```python
time_expanded = time[:, None, None]
x_t = time_expanded * noise + (1 - time_expanded) * actions   # t=1 is NOISE
u_t = noise - actions
```

Here `t = 1` is pure noise and `t = 0` is clean data, and then

```
d x_t / dt = ε − A  =  u_t     ✓ exactly the regressed target
```

so the code is self-consistent and its Euler update needs no sign fudge
(`modeling_smolvla.py:845,876`):

```python
dt = -1.0 / num_steps          # negative: integrate DOWN from t=1 to t=0
x_t = x_t + dt * v_t
```

**I am adopting the code's convention throughout this project** (`t=1` noise,
`t=0` data), because it makes `v_θ` literally the time-derivative of the path.
Both conventions train the identical network; they differ only in which end of
the interval you call "noise". Anyone reading the paper and the code
side-by-side will hit this, so it is worth having the answer ready.

### 4.1 The loss actually implemented

```
L(θ) = E ‖ v_θ(x_t, t, o) − (ε − A) ‖²         with  x_t = t ε + (1−t) A
```

where `o` is the VLM feature cache (images + language + state, read off at VLM
layer `N = 16`). In code, `F.mse_loss(u_t, v_t, reduction="none")`, then masked
by `action_is_pad` and averaged.

Note what is and is not conditioning: **the observation `o` enters only through
attention**, never through the interpolation. The path is defined purely on
actions. This is the same `p(A|O)`-not-`p(A,O)` design decision I noted for
Diffusion Policy: encode the observation once, outside the integration loop, and
cache it. That is why `sample_actions` builds the prefix KV cache *once* and then
runs 10 cheap expert-only steps (`modeling_smolvla.py:836-881`). The VLM, which
is the expensive part, runs exactly once per action chunk.

### 4.2 Timestep sampling: why Beta(1.5, 1.0), not Uniform

```python
beta_dist = torch.distributions.Beta(concentration1=1.5, concentration0=1.0)
time = beta_dist.sample((bsize,)) * 0.999 + 0.001
```

`Beta(α=1.5, β=1.0)` has density `∝ t^0.5` on `[0,1]`, so it is **increasing in
`t`**, i.e. it oversamples `t` near 1. Under the code's convention `t=1` is
*noise*, so this puts more training weight on the **high-noise end** of the path.

Why: at high noise the network has to make the hard global decision about which
mode of the action distribution to head toward, and errors there compound over
the entire remaining integration. At low noise the target is nearly a copy of
`x_t` and is easy. Uniform sampling wastes capacity on the easy end. The
`* 0.999 + 0.001` guard keeps `t` strictly inside `(0,1)` so neither endpoint is
degenerate.

(Careless reading trap: pi-0 samples from a Beta shifted toward *high noise* too,
but states it in the opposite time convention, so the α/β look swapped. Always
check which end of the interval the paper calls noise before comparing.)

## 5. Inference: integrating the ODE

```
x_1 ~ N(0, I)
for k in 0 .. N-1:
    t   = 1 − k/N
    v   = v_θ(x, t, o)          # o read from the cached prefix
    x  += (−1/N) · v
return x                        # ≈ a sample from p(A | O)
```

Plain forward Euler, uniform step size, `N = 10`. Three things worth noting:

- **Deterministic given the initial noise.** No stochasticity is injected during
  integration (unlike DDPM ancestral sampling). Same noise + same observation
  ⇒ same chunk. Useful for debugging; also means "fresh noise per replan" is the
  only source of plan-to-plan variation, which is precisely the jerk mechanism I
  diagnosed in the DP rollout.
- **Euler is enough only because the path is straight.** If the learned field
  were strongly curved, 10 Euler steps would accumulate visible discretisation
  error and I would need Heun/RK2 or more steps. Straightness is the training
  prior, not an inference assumption.
- **Cost per chunk** = 1 VLM forward (prefix, ~192 image tokens + language +
  state) + 10 action-expert forwards over 50 action tokens. The expert is
  `0.75 × d` wide and half-depth, so those 10 steps are cheap relative to the
  single VLM pass. That asymmetry is the entire efficiency argument of the paper.

## 6. Side-by-side with my DDPM build

| | Diffusion Policy (`Diffusion/`) | SmolVLA (`SmolVLA/`) |
|---|---|---|
| Path | stochastic, cosine beta schedule, 100 steps | deterministic straight line, no schedule |
| Interpolation | `x_t = √ᾱ_t x_0 + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε` | `x_t = (1−t) A + t ε` |
| Network predicts | `ε` (the noise) | `v = ε − A` (the velocity) |
| Target depends on `t`? | no (`ε`), but SNR weighting does | no, and no weighting either |
| Time sampling | uniform over `{0..99}` | `Beta(1.5, 1.0)`, biased to high noise |
| Inference | DDIM, 10 steps, curved path approximation | Euler, 10 steps, straight path |
| Clipping | `clip_sample=True`, `[-1,1]`, needs min-max norm | none; mean/std norm is fine |
| Conditioning | FiLM into every U-Net block | cross-attention to cached VLM KV |
| Denoiser body | 1D temporal U-Net | transformer, interleaved CA/SA |

**Why `v`-prediction here but `ε`-prediction there.** In DDPM, `ε`, `x_0` and `v`
parameterisations are related by invertible, `t`-dependent affine maps, and
`ε`-prediction wins empirically because it keeps the target's scale roughly
constant across the schedule. In flow matching there is no schedule: `v = ε − A`
is *the* natural object because it is literally the ODE's right-hand side. Note
you can trivially read off the equivalent `x_0`-prediction:
`Â = x_t − t · v_θ`. That identity is handy for debugging (project the predicted
velocity to an action chunk at any `t` and see whether it looks like a
trajectory).
